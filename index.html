~/portfolio: cat engineering-principles.txt
  1. 01

    Evaluate before optimizing

    A high score is meaningless if the evaluation setup is wrong.

  2. 02

    Prevent leakage

    Features unavailable at prediction time do not belong in the model.

  3. 03

    Measure retrieval

    RAG quality needs retrieval metrics, not just a chatbot demo.

  4. 04

    Build the service

    A model isn't the product. APIs, databases and interfaces matter.

  5. 05

    Fail explicitly

    When confidence is low, refusing is better than hallucinating.

Case study 1 · ML · 2026

Bank Marketing Campaign Response Prediction

The interesting part wasn't the model. It was finding the leakage.

I built an end-to-end classification pipeline to predict whether a client would respond to a marketing campaign.

During evaluation, including call duration pushed ROC-AUC from 0.803 to about 0.93. The problem is that call duration is only known after the campaign interaction. It cannot be used when deciding which customer to contact.

I treated this as target leakage, removed the feature, and rebuilt the evaluation pipeline.

final model
roc_auc     0.803
pr_auc      0.456
validation  5-fold stratified CV
models      CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost,
            Random Forest, Logistic Regression

The final CatBoost model is served through a Streamlit application with per-prediction feature importance.

LessonA better metric isn't necessarily a better model.

Case study 2 · AI · RAG

Engineering Copilot

A RAG system for searching and answering questions over automotive engineering documents.

The system processes engineering PDFs into section-aware chunks, generates embeddings, and retrieves relevant passages using vector similarity. Answers include document, section and page citations rather than returning unsupported text.

I also added an explicit refusal mechanism. When retrieval confidence falls below a threshold, the system refuses to answer instead of guessing.

Explainable multimodal AI for acute leukemia diagnosis

One explainable model that reads every modality together, instead of a separate model for each.

The research is developing an AI-enabled multimodal digital hematopathology platform for early diagnosis and genomic classification of acute leukemia (hematolymphoid malignancies). It combines CBC parameters, digital morphology, flow cytometry, cytogenetics and molecular genetics in a single model, for both morphologic and genomic classification.

I am starting with the literature on how leukemia diagnosis has evolved and how AI is applied to CBC parameters, digital morphology and flow cytometry.
